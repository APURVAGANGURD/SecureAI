Learn about public Wi-Fi risks and how to protect yourself on untrusted networks

Public Wi-Fi Risks

Man-in-the-Middle Attacks

Attackers intercept communications between you and websites to steal data.

Example: Fake Wi-Fi hotspot at a coffee shop

Packet Sniffing

Tools capture unencrypted data sent over the network.

Example: Reading unencrypted email or messages

Evil Twin Attacks

Fake access points with legitimate-sounding names.

Example: "Airport_Free_WiFi" vs real "Airport-Free-WiFi"

Malware Distribution

Infected networks that push malware to connected devices.

Example: Fake update prompts while browsing

Protection Strategies

Essential Protection

Must Do
  • Use a reputable VPN service
  • Verify network names with staff
  • Disable auto-connect features
  • Turn off file sharing

Recommended Practices

Should Do
  • Use HTTPS Everywhere browser extension
  • Enable firewall on your device
  • Use cellular data for sensitive activities
  • Forget networks after use

Advanced Security

Extra Protection
  • Use a privacy-focused DNS
  • Set up a travel router with VPN
  • Use separate browser profiles
  • Consider using a mobile hotspot

Device Settings Checklist

What to Do If Compromised

1

Disconnect Immediately

Turn off Wi-Fi and disconnect from the network

2

Change Passwords

Change passwords for any accounts accessed

3

Check Accounts

Look for suspicious activity in financial and email accounts

4

Run Security Scan

Scan device for malware or keyloggers

5

Enable 2FA

Add two-factor authentication to important accounts

6

Monitor Credit

Watch for unusual credit activity if financial info was exposed

Travel Security Tips

Use Your Own Hotspot

Consider a portable Wi-Fi hotspot or use smartphone tethering

Hotel Wi-Fi Caution

Hotel networks are prime targets. Always use VPN.

Airport & Airplane Wi-Fi

Treat all airport and in-flight Wi-Fi as potentially compromised

Travel Devices

Consider using a separate device for travel with minimal personal data
