Website Safety Education

Understanding HTTPS

What is HTTPS?

HTTPS (Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure) encrypts data between your browser and the website, protecting it from interception by hackers.

Look for the Padlock

A padlock icon in the address bar indicates HTTPS. Click it to view security certificate details and verify the site's identity.

HTTPS ≠ Complete Security

HTTPS only encrypts data in transit. It doesn't mean the website itself is trustworthy or safe from malware.

Common Website Threats

Phishing Sites

Fake websites designed to steal login credentials by mimicking legitimate sites like banks or social media.

How to Spot:
  • Check URL spelling carefully
  • Look for poor design/grammar
  • Verify through official apps
  • Watch for urgency tactics

Malware Distribution

Sites that automatically download malicious software or trick you into downloading viruses and ransomware.

Protection:
  • Avoid "urgent" update prompts
  • Download from official sources only
  • Use reputable antivirus software
  • Keep software updated

Malvertising

Malicious ads that can infect your device with malware even without clicking, through "drive-by downloads".

Prevention:
  • Use ad-blockers (uBlock Origin)
  • Keep browsers updated
  • Use script blockers
  • Avoid shady websites

Safe Browsing Checklist

Check for HTTPS and padlock icon

Always look for https:// and the padlock symbol in the address bar

Verify domain name spelling

Check for typosquatting: faceb00k.com instead of facebook.com

Look for contact information

Legitimate sites usually have clear contact details and privacy policies

Check for trust seals (verify them)

Click on trust seals to ensure they're legitimate and not just images

Read privacy policy if submitting data

Understand how your data will be used and protected

Use bookmarks for important sites

Bookmark legitimate sites to avoid typing URLs and falling for phishing

Enable browser security features

Turn on safe browsing, pop-up blockers, and phishing protection

Keep browser and plugins updated

Regular updates patch security vulnerabilities

What to Do If You Visit a Malicious Site

1

Don't Enter Any Information

Close the tab immediately without clicking anything or entering data.

2

Clear Browser Data

Clear cookies, cache, and browsing history for the affected site.

3

Run Security Scan

Scan your device with antivirus/anti-malware software immediately.

4

Change Passwords

If you entered any credentials, change those passwords immediately on a secure device.

5

Report the Site

Report phishing sites to Google Safe Browsing and the legitimate company being impersonated.

6

Monitor Accounts

Watch for suspicious activity on any accounts you may have accessed.

Advanced URL Analysis Tips

URL Structure Analysis

  • Check subdomains: Too many (login.paypal.security.site.com) is suspicious
  • Watch for @ symbols: http://paypal.com@malicious.com tricks browsers
  • Look for encoded characters: %20 for space, %40 for @ in phishing URLs
  • Verify TLDs: .com is common, .xyz or .top can be suspicious

Hover Before Clicking

  • Hover over links to see actual destination URL
  • Check if displayed text matches actual link
  • Look for URL shorteners (bit.ly, tinyurl) - they hide destination
  • Right-click and "Copy link address" to inspect

SSL Certificate Checks

  • Click padlock to view certificate details
  • Check certificate issuer and validity dates
  • Look for Extended Validation (EV) certificates
  • Verify certificate matches domain name
