Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) Guide

Learn about 2FA/MFA and how to secure your accounts with an extra layer of protection

What is Two-Factor Authentication?

Two-Factor Authentication (2FA) adds an extra layer of security to your accounts by requiring two forms of verification:

Something You Know

Your password or PIN

Something You Have

Your phone, security key, or authenticator app

Something You Are

Biometrics like fingerprint or facial recognition

How to Enable 2FA

1

Choose Your Method

Select between authenticator app (recommended), SMS, or security key

2

Go to Account Settings

Navigate to Security or Privacy settings in your account

3

Enable 2FA

Follow the prompts to set up your chosen 2FA method

4

Save Backup Codes

Download or write down backup codes in case you lose access

5

Test It

Log out and log back in to ensure it works correctly

Backup Codes & Recovery

Always Save Backup Codes

When enabling 2FA, you'll receive 8-10 backup codes. Save them in a secure location (password manager or printed copy). These codes are your lifeline if you lose access to your 2FA device.

Multiple Devices

Some authenticator apps (like Authy) allow multi-device sync. Enable this feature to access your codes from multiple devices.

Recovery Email/Phone

Keep your recovery email and phone number updated. This is often the only way to regain access if you lose everything.

Priority Accounts for 2FA

Critical (Enable Immediately)

  • Email accounts (Gmail, Outlook, etc.)
  • Banking and financial services
  • Password manager
  • Social media with personal data

Important (Enable Soon)

  • Cloud storage (Google Drive, Dropbox)
  • Online shopping (Amazon, eBay)
  • Government services
  • Health portals

Recommended (Enable When Possible)

  • Streaming services
  • Gaming accounts
  • Work-related tools
  • Educational platforms
