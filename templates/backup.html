Secure Data Backup Planner

Protect your data from ransomware, hardware failure, and accidental loss

Why Backups Are Critical

Ransomware

Malicious software that encrypts your files and demands payment for decryption.

Attack every 11 seconds worldwide

Hardware Failure

All storage devices eventually fail. Average HDD lifespan: 3-5 years.

140,000 hard drives fail weekly in the US

Human Error

Accidental deletion, formatting, or overwriting of important files.

29% of data loss is caused by human error

Physical Damage

Theft, fire, flood, or other physical damage to your devices.

43% of companies that experience major data loss never reopen

The 3-2-1 Backup Rule

3

Three Copies

Keep at least three copies of your data

  • Original file
  • Local backup
  • Offsite backup
2

Two Different Media

Use two different types of storage media

  • External hard drive
  • NAS (Network Attached Storage)
  • SSD (Solid State Drive)
  • Tape (for enterprises)
1

One Offsite Copy

Keep one copy in a different physical location

  • Cloud storage
  • Bank safety deposit box
  • Friend/relative's house
  • Office (if home backup)

Backup Solutions Comparison

Cloud Backup

Pros:

  • Automatic and continuous
  • Accessible from anywhere
  • Protects against local disasters
  • Usually encrypted

Cons:

  • Monthly/annual fees
  • Slow initial backup
  • Limited by internet speed
  • Privacy concerns

Recommended Services:

  • Backblaze
  • iDrive
  • CrashPlan
  • Google One
Cost: $5-10/month for personal use

External Hard Drive

Pros:

  • One-time purchase
  • Fast backup/restore
  • Complete control
  • No internet required

Cons:

  • Manual process
  • Can be lost/stolen
  • Physical failure risk
  • No offsite protection

Recommended Products:

  • WD My Passport
  • Seagate Backup Plus
  • Samsung T5/T7 SSD
  • LaCie Rugged
Cost: $60-200 one-time

NAS (Network Storage)

Pros:

  • Centralized for multiple devices
  • Automated backups
  • Redundancy (RAID)
  • Remote access possible

Cons:

  • Higher upfront cost
  • Technical setup required
  • Still local (needs cloud sync)
  • Power consumption

Recommended Brands:

  • Synology
  • QNAP
  • WD My Cloud
  • Asustor
Cost: $200-1000+ initial

Disaster Recovery Plan

1

Identify Critical Data

What absolutely must be recovered first? (Financial documents, client data, etc.)

2

Establish Recovery Time Objective (RTO)

How quickly do you need to be back up and running?

  • Personal: 24-48 hours
  • Business: 1-8 hours
  • Critical: Minutes to 1 hour
3

Test Your Backups

Regularly test restore process. A backup isn't valid until it's been successfully restored.

Test Schedule:

Personal: Every 3-6 months

Business: Monthly or quarterly

4

Document the Process

Create written instructions for recovery. Include:

  • Backup locations and passwords
  • Software installation steps
  • Contact information for support
  • Priority restoration order

Backup Encryption

Why Encrypt Backups?

Protects sensitive data if backup media is lost or stolen. Especially important for cloud backups and portable drives.

Encryption Tools

  • VeraCrypt: Free, open source disk encryption
  • BitLocker: Built into Windows Pro/Enterprise
  • FileVault 2: Built into macOS
  • Backup software: Many include encryption options

Password Management

Store encryption passwords in a password manager. Consider sharing with a trusted person in case of emergency.
